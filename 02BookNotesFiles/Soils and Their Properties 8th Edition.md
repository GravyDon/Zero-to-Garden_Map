## Overview of current understanding
The soil is a complex matrix of minerals that exhibit mutliple interesting properties which account for their porous structure. The composition of the soils is distinguished into particulate size, whose phenotype tends to respond to different mineralogical materials. The composition of a soil within a particular region depends upon multiple factors which relate to the chemical potential that are resultant of the particular situation on Earth. Climate, parent material, Surrounding topography determined over ages of Earth's development, erosion factors, native species, and likely more. 

The book sticks on the edaphology of the living structure that is soil, of which microorganisms play an essential role in the exchange of ions in the **colloidal solution** that is the soil. 

It cannot be stressed enough to the density of the solution that occupies the soil and how it changes as a result of introduced water. Keeping the solution at an **ideal concentration** is the difference between a plant with sensitive root systems living and dying; **the concentration of ions in solution**, and its ratio with air within the **internal suface area of the soil** is key.

The utilization of the surrounding colloidal solution, that is contained within soil structure, is an essential element in plant development that is 50% dependent on the mineralogical properties of the soil and 50% dependent on the organic properties of the soil.
