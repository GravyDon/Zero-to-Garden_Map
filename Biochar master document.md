## Biochar creation processes
---
 
Biochar pyrolysis can be differentiated by temperature, atmospheric conditions, and feedstocks within the biochar chamber. 
##### Temperature differentiation
- Slow pyrolysis (temperatures of 250 - 400C) -> lower porosity but higher ration of carbon double bonds with carbon and nitrogen for nutrient fixation -> more suitable as soil amendment for fertility
- Fast pyrolysis (400 - 900+C) -> higher porosity -> greater potential for filtration and heavy metal and recalcitrant pollutant sequestration

##### Atmospheric conditions
- Gasification
- Steam pyrolysis or anhydrous
- Carbonisation

## Biochar in ecological remediation processes

For creating soils that are rich in biochar, it is essential to process the biochar coming out of these processes based on the intended purpose in relation to ecological remediation. The process of creation is also linked to the ideal/advantaged use case (refer to [[Biochar master document#Table 1|Table]])

For use in soils, it is essential to ensure that the added biochar is...
- Inoculated with bacteria and/or fungi
- At a pH that is suitable for growth in the soil when interacting with water
- Has sufficient pore sizes and existent bonds that can sequester nutrients at a sufficient concentration to maintain fertility over a long period when given fertilizer. it should be possible to design an experiment measuring the cation exchange capacity with the conductivity meter, a saturated solution of water, and a piece of biochar to absorb cations in the solution. It will give a relative measure of exchange capacity since it will not be to the standards that are likely required for scientific communications.
- Is made from a feedstock which results in a high CEC ratio and a suitable Ph for amending (alkaline)

## Biochar experiment 1
Since I already have a charcoal sample ready, I'm now going to prepare them for soil introduction through the preparing of the grains. For this, I'm going to use a 100 mesh filter screen, which will let through grains that might otherwise be too small to effect drainage in the soil, and then I'm going to apply a 35 mesh screen to only get particulates between the sizes of 0.15 and 0.5 (refer to[[Biochar master document#Table 2|table 2]]). This will allow for the soil to still have aggregate sizes that make sense while breaking up the aggregates with certain pore sizes (hypothesis 1). The degree of aeration will directly correlate with the ratio of biochar added (hypothesis 2). Woody feedstocks that are made with lignin and other firm structures, when carbonized, will provide the most aeration while crumbly and leafy feedstocks will provide poor aeration (hypothesis 3).

The experiment that I want to perform will be the creation of soil blocks or standardized volumes of soil. The mixture ratio of native soil to biochar will be by weight at ~0% moisture. A solar oven might need to be employed for the preparation of the native soil for this or a microwave. 

After mixing the soil block, the resultant will be put into a chamber that will both only let water through the soil block and only let air through the soil block. This will require a chamber that is specially fitted to the dimensions of the soil block to prevent air or water from migrating on the sides of the soil block OR special fittings to limit the available interface to only the soil block. 

The following tests will be done on the biochar alone:
1. CEC (as a large aggregate piece from a pyrolysis sample)
2. Ph
3. Particle size (controlled)

The following tests will be done on the soil block with X w% biochar 
1. Drainage test (simple)
2. Aeration test (harder)
3. Ph
4. Moisture level at field capacity
5. Moisture lost from saturation to field capacity
6. CEC

The point of this test is to confirm experimental studies on the efficacy of biochar in remediating unfavorable properties of clay soils such as aeration and drainage while also maintaining high CEC content and buffering the Ph. Adding microbiota into the soil via pore size inclusion is a bonus!

### Biochar soil integration idea
[[Vermicomposting Project]] connection:

It might be easier to introduce the biochar into a soil surrogate before integration into the existing soil profile. The obvious system to introduce into would be a worm bin complete with inoculated bacterial populations, detrivores existent from a soil inoculant, and the presence of seedling species. 

Introduction of nitrogenous material, with the already included microbial species, will accelerate decomposition rate with the biochar being properly adapted via the saturation of microbial populations over successive generations.
