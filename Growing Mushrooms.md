## Substrate Preparation and Inoculation

The first and most important step is creating the substrate for the mycelium to decompose. This step is deceptively simple; there are many, many options available in the creation of a suitable substrate for the mycelium to grow in. However, depending on the strain of mushroom, different levels of success will be found with different substrates and growing conditions. Thus, it is important to experiment with different substrates to ascertain which species of mushroom prefers which substrates. 

The following four substrates provided will suffice to give you flexibility in the obtaining of materials as well as the methods of preparation. Note that these are the full recipes, but each recipe will require the following common items: 
1. Mason jars (at least a 1/2 pint[^1])

```tabs

Materials: 
- Brown rice flour (BRF)
- Vermiculite
- Water
ratio of BRF:Verm:H20 is 1:2:1

Methods
--- 2. Rye berries
Materials: 
- Organically sourced rye berries
-  Water

--- 3. Liquid media
Materials: 
- Karo light/dark corn syrup
- Water
  
--- 4. Agar media
```


| Substrate           | Pros                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Cons                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| BRF                 | Accessible materials; most documented method; simple;                                                                                                                                                                  | Can get pretty messy; harder to extract from jars without a wide mouth.                                                                                                         |
| Rye berries         | Easiest method and materials; easy to breakup final result for minimized colonization time prior to fruiting stage; can be grown in any jar form factor; can easily make bulk for large inocolum                       | Harder to sterilize; Must source seeds correctly to prevent fungicide interference;                                                                                             |
| corn syrup solution | Most accessible materials; versatile result; cooler than the rest; easy to inoculate                                                                                                                                   | Does not provide structure for the mycelium. No good for making a lot of jars.                                                                                                  |
| Agar media          | Great for starting other inoculation media; fast; growth restricted to 1-plane (easily observable results); can easily make larger inoculum; least likely to be contaminated if handled properly; easiest to inoculate | Can be difficult to re-inoculate certain media if not applied correctly; growth restricted to 1-plane (less mycelial growth to proliferate); Materials will run a higher price. |


[^1]: Only obtain a size jar that can be accomodated within your pressure cooker
