### Main idea
The ecology provides a basis for life to exist on top of. For the sake of animals, this creates a unity with life that has been taken away from the average human experience greatly. This is a result of the rapid, degrading process of urbanization on natural resources, the detriment of with has grave consequences for the health and happiness of the human population[^1]. When we prioritize the patterning of a community, one that has been allowed to instantiate itself through the processes of natural selection, and its implication in competition for resource optimization, the role of the gardener becomes to create abundance in accordance with a set of natural and anthropogenic rules on top of the creation of (pseudo)natural environments. 

The fundamental premise that this is anthropogenically advantageous, which a proper strategy should be mainly focused towards the attainment of suitable gains to work input, while still essentially emulating a mostly natural ruleset[^2]. This natural ruleset is done for a different purpose within the design considerations, which is not counterpoised to the main purpose of . The ultimate goal of creating a new/refurbished agricultural system is to keep systems robust against neglect. Resources should be circulated and stored by the ecosystem to ensure that species with high yield can grow. An antithetical approach to this is easily observed in the purely anthropogenic forced agro-industries that do not consider transitions between soil states but simply try to force mediums to produce hyper-specific, logistically/productivity/biomass maximized within extremely specific niches that can consitute suitable farmland (being exceptionally flat, a loam texture or preferred for a cash-crop such a silt and soybeans)

On the side of the gardener, this consideration towards environmental stability of the niches being embodied into your garden (multiple being preferable upon surveying of the surrounding area), is observable in the ordering of different genera of plant, animal, fungal, bacterial, and protozoan forms of life across the given piece of land/property. The sharing of resources can be maximized in such a way as to provide more resources in an feed-back loop effect that is self-regulating. Creating a robust ecology will only allow suitably compatible plants and animals to exist in a space; a properly intentioned orientation of these in a given space, in a balance with the available resources, interactions between other created niches, climate, effect of continual existence with humans, and the compatibility of particular plants within the formal space will all determine whether or not the particular ecology has entered an entropic state that is sustainable or will reach a level of sustainability that is a stable configuration of reasoned plant selections.

These plant selections, and their arrangement in accordance with the resources that are available to the given site in time, thus form the crucial hinge upon which the CORE of this educational process hopes to activate people for. 

### Phases of understanding
Pragmatically, grounding our education in both theory and action is essential. The practices that constitute the creation of a garden can be divided into four different **modes** of action:
- **Introduction of material**
	- Topsoil amendments:
		Leaves, mulch
	    Biodigester liquid fertilizer
	- Soil amendments
		Vermicomposting
		Biochar
		Biodigester manure
		Loam texture initialization (if applicable to the given plant community)
		Ph balancing materials
		Microbiomes (for plants with symbiotic bacterial systems)
- **Growing of material**
	- Growing plants &/or food + medicines, animals &/or livestock, and ecologies
- **Directing of resources flows**
	- Irrigation and water penetration into the soil
	- sunlight distribution
	- utilization of wind, topologies, and geological features
	- soil nutrient stabilization and delivery to root systems; prevention of leeching
	- heat change buffering and optimization (not as important)
- **Processing of material (input and output)**
	- Creation of inputs
	- Harvesting, preservation, and processing of inputs into usable forms.
