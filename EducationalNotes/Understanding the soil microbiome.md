The soil microbiome is one of the most important variables to consider in the creation of a garden. It's constituted with archeabacteria, bacteria, cyanobacteria, arbuscular mycohorrizal fungi, single-celled fungi,  detrivores, protists, and of course also meso/marco fauna, like worms, which interact closely with the microbiome.

Their presence in the soil is in constant flux in accordance with their rate of proliferation, presence and concentration of predators, pests, and competition for incorporated organic matter, and other environmental factors such as temperature, Ph, moisture content, and pore sizes in the soil. 

## Importance and mechanism behind cultivation of a healthy soil microbiome

In the cycling of nutrients into the soil, microbiota serve as an essential trophic level taking organic matter and mobilizing it via enzyme allowed metabolism, removal of heavy metals through chelation, and generally catabolic action on soil residues resulting from death and subsequent immobilization of matter from higher trophic levels. 

![](https://i.gyazo.com/ac04217ab88c98ed11efe59fa9d92326.png)

In the figure above, the movement of matter between sources and sinks is illustrated. Microbiota act in the following manner to mobilize nutrients within the biosphere (green sphere):
- **Respiration** (intake of CO2 or O2 dependent on species and exhalation of O2, CO2, and other gaseous species) -> interaction with atmosphere
- **Chelation of heavy metals** -> uptake of heavy metals from the soil
- **Enzyme and phytohormone production** -> modification of biosphere capacity to uptake from available nutrient sources
- **Mechanical mixing of soil layers** in the case of earthworms and some detrivores
- **Mineralization of carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorous** via catabolism and immobilization in waste byproducts.

Other roles played by the microbial biomass are:
- **Competition with diseases and antagonizing of pests** when competing for available carboniferous reserves
- **Processing of potentially deleterious compounds** such as ammonia and sulfur dioxide (raising Ph and facilitation oxidative stress response), *but only at acceptable concentrations*.
