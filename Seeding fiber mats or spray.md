Just though of an interesting idea while reading the art of weaving. What if it were possible to create a fiber matrix that could be used to seed life within the existing soil biome. Would have to be used in conjunction with a local culture and a naturalized, introduced species (such as a fungi or bacteria. It is advisable not to introduce an insect). 

# Purpose
The fiber mat acts as the substrate for growth and minerals to attach. Inoculants can be added via microbiological or mycological methods involving teasing of the fiber matrix to then introduce organisms into the vulnerable internal structure of the fiber. The functional purpose of this structure is to provide the following functions in the seeding process:
1. Ubiquitous introduction of a spread of microorganisms into the soil 
2. Erosion control via the introduction of species that will bind with the surface soil via proteins (mycology bias)
3. Provide an organic humus once fully decomposed
4. Be doped in solutions of fertilizer, essential ions, Ph treatment, or chemicals 

This could be a way to rapidly introduce microorganisms into an area via a surface treatment. The surface treatment in question depends strongly on the application method chosen.
