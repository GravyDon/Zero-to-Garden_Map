## Phase 1: Getting the charcoal
##### Experiment 1: Using a rocket stove for the heating element
The rocket stove that was used was purchased off of Amazon. The hypothesis was that, even though the fuel load and the interface between the metal can was minimized, the nature of the rocket stove, being able to pull in a lot of oxygen for combustion, would allow for the fuel to burn very hot which would result in an increased rate of combustion and funneling into the bottom of the can reaching a heat flux that would be able to completely pyrolyse the charcoal stock (wood)

This did not work. After about 5 hours of tending the fire, and the usage of three whole harbor freight bags of small to large branches + some larger chunks, the only result was some beginnings of charification on the bottom of the wood or wood in contact with the can interface with the rocket stove output. This was a disappointing result; water was still boiling out of the wood xylems indicating the pyrolysis still had not occurred. 

##### Experiment 2: Nested can in an industrial metal barrel
Thankfully, the stars aligned and I was serendipitous enough to finally have a place where I could burn shit in a metal can. How lucky!

This was a huge success! Not only was the amount of fuel used less (in terms of volume; not sure about mass. Required a 30 gallon bin to be filled with wood for fuel), but the process was much faster (<1 hour till completion). The byproduct was a full can of charcoal that is waiting to be broken into smaller chunks for inoculation!

Before beginning the experiment, you must modify the barrel and can in the following ways:
1. The barrel needs air flow from the bottom, so drill 3/4 inch holes into the sides of the metal barrel. This can be achieved with a hole saw and a drill. If these are no accessible to you, the consider instead raising the can off of the ground with bricks and drilling smaller holes into the bottom of the barrel instead
2. The metal can also needs holes but in the top of the lid. These should be small holes (1/4 - 1/2 inch). I put 5 holes in the top of my can. These are for the purpose of letting steam and hydrocarbons escape during the charcoal making process.
#### Experiment 2 Methods
Prepare the fire by cutting firewood to size and making a suitable fire lay. The fire lay does not have to be advanced just something that will burn small pieces to a flame suitable to combust large pieces. I made a simple fire lay with small pieces piled together with larger pieces surrounding the center and a tepee of medium branches straddling the center (which would ignite once the flame got hot and high enough). Make sure that the can can be nested within the barrel without disrupting the fire lay. I had to put the can off-center because of this, but this turned out to be minimal in its effect, but could have caused bigger issues. 

Use the lighter fluid and fire starter to begin the fire. 

Place the trammel and can containing the wood stock over the fire making sure that the can is as close to the fire lay without disrupting it. The fire should be able to wrap around the can at its peak and produce large amounts of steam/wood gas from the metal can

Tend to the fire ensuring that it stays hot. Having a metal rod that can act like a little chute for firewood makes the process of adding more firewood to the system simple and fun!

Observe the byproducts coming out of the metal can to determine when to take the can off of the fire. At first, the can will produce steam. This will be obvious from the bubbling coming out of the holes and the lack of combustion of the gas when touched by the roaring fire. After this, wood gas will be produced which is toxic and flammable. DO NOT INHALE! This gas will combust in contact with the fire. It is possible to eventually see a complete reduction in the production of any gases from the can which means your charcoal is completely cooked. **Further cooking will produce diminishing returns, but might lead to activated charcoal with enough heat and the introduction of water into the process (UNTESTED)**. 

(optional) consider rotating the can to maximize exposure of the can to the flames. This was done once during my experimental phase. 

**Use the metal rod and your gloves to take the can and trammel off of the barrel.** Quickly cool the can in the event of combustion occurring after taking the can off of the barrel. This occurs due to new oxygen being able to enter the system through the imperfect seal on the can. If allowed to continue, there might be some losses due to combusted charcoal turning into ash. **Transport your finished can to staging for the next phase: crushing and inoculation**

Let the fire burn out and then collect the ash. [[01Action plans#Action item 14 Seed Starting II Liquid amendments|This can be used for a fertilizer in a different process]].
