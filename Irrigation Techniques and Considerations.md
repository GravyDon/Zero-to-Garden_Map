01: Irrigation can cause salination to the soil if not careful/judiscious about the type of irrigation and its usage 
- #irrigration/Drip irrigation can cause a "bulb-shaped" pattern of salination emanating out from the drip irrigation site. **This can cause high salination both at the edges of the radius on the top and deep into the soil**. [[Irrigation Techniques and Considerations#Appendix A Effects of Salination on Soil Abridged|Rainfall can cause accumulated salts on the surface to penetrate root zone causing damage to the soil structure and water stress due to osmotic pressure differentials across root membrane]].


#### Appendix A: Effects of Salination on Soil Abridged
Any type of salt in the soil attracts water due to hydrogen bonding. The phenomenon of osmotic pressure requires that roots must balance the number of solutes on the internal root layer with the soil so as to take in more water for plant processing. **It is essential to account for plant salt tolerance to ensure that the root system can intake enough water to support plant synthesis.**
	Signs of salt stress can resemble water stress with:
	- singed leaves
	- curled leaves
	- signs of dessication

Salts in the soil have multiple effects depending on charge. For example, Clay usually carries a negative charge. Thus, monovalent salts, such as sodium chloride, will cause segregation of bonded clay particles (refer to figure below). This is in opposition to divalent salts which will cause soil flocculation which is beneficial for soil drainage. **Thus, adding divalent salts could reduce total salt concentration due to better drainage!?!?**
