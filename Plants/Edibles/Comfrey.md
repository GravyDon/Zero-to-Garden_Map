## Uses


As a food, the leaves can be boiled at a young age (before they develop fuzz). The shoots can be blanched similar to asparagus. This is not recommended though as the compounds contained within Comfrey are hepatoxic in large doses.

Roasted roots with dandelion and chicory roots can be used to make coffee **WARNING: Chronic consumption of comfrey may damage the liver. be careful and only use on acute bases**

##### Medicinal properties from PFAF
Comfrey is exceedingly useful as a general care for skin and wounds when applied a s poultice. Has been known to treat: cuts bruises, sprains, sores, eczema, varicose veings, broken bones. Internally, comfrey can help with internal bleeding. Active alkaloid is allantoin a cell proliferant that spees up the healing process (from PFAF). The allantoin in the plant acts to proliferate new cellular growth. Rosemarinic acid relieves pain and inflammation.

## Ecological Functions
Can drag up a lot of phosphorous and other trace minerals in the soil upwards. The accumulated material in the leaves can then be used to mend the soil or even for processing via bacteria, mycelium, or through other means to return bio-available forms of phosphorous and nitrogen to enter the soil

**Usage as green manure is most useful later in the growing season of the main yield of the garden since phosphorous is more important in making the fruits bigger.**
