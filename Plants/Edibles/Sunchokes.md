### Description 
Height: up to 10ft tall by some sources!! PFAF: 2.4 - 0.6m
Daisy like blooms on tall, leafy stalks. Leaves are lanceolate with clustering of sunchokes being common due to how the roots proliferate. Bloom is similar in shape to a sunflower but smaller and more numerous on each stalk.

### How to Grow
Noted to require minimal care; very easy to grow with little maintanence. Mulching is the best thing you can do since the water needs are high due to the biomass.
Temperatures above 86F tend to cause physiological stress and damage the plant.

##### Harvesting
**Harvest tubers when they are about 10-15cm long with plenty of underground stems.**
Ideal Harvest time: late fall or early winter (when the ground freezes, the tubers will provide a sweet sap)
- Harvest Period: Early winter or Late winter

Tubers will keep in the winter soil well and also become sweeter! This can help to improve soil quality and keep the space in the soil before spring plantings
##### Propagation and control
Propagation: Divide out tubers to recommended size and replant.
Control: Due to their aggressive growth, it is best to grow these in pots or demarcated sections of a garden, as they can easily shade out other plants due to their size and density. **due to their tubers sending out runners, it is quite possible that they can easily escape a bed and spread radially from any missed tubers.**

### Seasonal Periods

Blooming time: Late summer (July) to late fall

## Uses
Due to the high biomass, the roots can be useful as a bulk food as well as a feed stock for the creation of alcohol (reportedly sweeter than alcohol coming from sugar beets) (PFAF)
**IT IS IMPERATIVE to protect the tubers during the growth season and after as they are a favorite target for slugs**

The Inulin in them can be used to heal your gut microbiome as a prebiotic.
