### How to Grow

Native habitat is that of Eastern and central united states in wet - dry prairies and open wooded areas [[Echinacea#Sources|Wikipedia]]

A robust plant that is simple to grow requiring minimal care.

##### Soil preparation
- Water need: moist soil, but is drought tolerant once established [[Echinacea#Sources|NCGT]]
- Sun needs: full sun 
- Ph Level: 5.5 - 7.5

##### Germination
**Need cold stratification to successfully germinate**
- Ideal germination temp: 65 - 70F

##### Sowing
- Sowing time: fall or spring (with cold stratification)
- Sowing depth: 1/4in
- Sowing spacing: 24-36 inches
##### Harvesting
Ideal Harvest time: depends on flower development OR if root is of interest, then at the end of growing season
- Harvest Period: for cut flowers, throughout the summer and early fall. For the root, at the end of the growing period once the temperatures become colder (sap will retreat to the root)
##### Propagation and control
Propagation: Propagates through seed
Control: Cut seed heads before they release seeds. The seed heads form from pollinated flower heads, so it is possible to quell seeds simply by cutting flowers before they form seed heads

##### Medicinal properties from PFAF
Supposedly one of the most widely used plants in medicinal formulations. In Germany "over 200 pharmaceutical preparations are made from echinacea". 

One of the most widely used herbs in Native American medicine. infusions can be used to treat wounds, bites, and sores. Antiseptic properties assist in healing

Root is an adaptogen, alterative, antiseptic, aphrodisiac, depurative, diaphoretic, digestive, and sialogogue

## Ecological functions
- Nectary source for pollinators throughout the season
- Native pollinators overwinter in the hollow stems
- Produces seed that native birds eat up!
- Deer, heat, humidity, and salt resistant making it great for remediation!
