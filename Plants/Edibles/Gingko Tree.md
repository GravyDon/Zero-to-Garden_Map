### How to Grow

##### Soil preparation
- Water need: can survive dry conditions (0.5 inch per week)
- Sun needs: full sun
- Ph Level: 5 - 7.5

##### Germination
- Ideal germination temp: NA

##### Sowing
- Sowing time: Fall 
- Sowing depth: 1/4in
- Sowing spacing: 15m (accounting for rhizosphere overlap)
Best grown in pots in a controlled environment due to their scarcity and vulnerability when young
##### Harvesting
Ideal Harvest time: Anytime for the greens; Spring for the flowers; fall for the fruits.
- Harvest Period: Varies depending on the part being harvested.
##### Propagation and control
Propagation: 
1. By seed. The tree takes a long time to grow and a long time for fruits to be born of female trees (35 years of development). Thus, the potential for replication of a gingko tree is slim without a growing stock available. **Pollinated by the wind**
2. Cuttings. Half-ripe wood (with exposed green growth) about 15cm long can be placed in a sandy frame common to rooting cuttings.
Control: **Slow growing**

**Grafting trick**: It is possible to graft a male branch to a female tree to pollinate the WHOLE tree! This results in a large harvest without having to rely on the wind to bring your fortune of gingko nuts.

### Seasonal Periods

Blooming time: Summer - early-fall

## Uses
Can be used as a brain tonic. The fruits and seeds are good cooked, but beware eating raw since there is a chemical which destroys vitamin B6 in the body if not cooked.

The circulatory enhancement has been reported to improve memory. This is helpful to treat disease of age as well as general forgetfulness

##### Medicinal properties from PFAF
Tonic for the brain through increasing circulation to the peripheral nerves (grey matter). Also proved to increase general circulation in the peripheries.

Chemicals in the leaves, knowns as gingkoglides, are effective in treating asthma and other allergic responses that are non-life threatening (not an adrenaline alternative). A daily application can likely help with allergies.
