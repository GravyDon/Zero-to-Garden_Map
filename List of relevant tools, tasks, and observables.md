This is a list of tools that is connected to a list of observables and actions. This will let you know what these tools are and how they relate to each other and the world in which they are contextualized. **By no means is this list comprehensive and does not envision the scale of labor that is required for maintaining a medium to large sized, intensive farm**
## Tasks (chronologically ordered in a season)
- [[List of relevant tools, tasks, and observables#Ambient Observables|Measuring]] (winter)
- Digging (winter and throughout season)
- Building (winter)
- Soil amending (pre-spring)
- Watering (pre-spring - fall)
- Planting seeds (Spring, Summer, fall)
- Transplanting (Spring, summer)
- Fertilizing (Spring; throughout season without excess)
- Pruning (Spring; throughout season without excess UNLESS plants are fine with a lot of cutting)
- Harvesting (Late-spring, Summer, Fall, Winter)
- Drying (fall, winter)
- Canning (fall, winter)
- Preserving (fall, winter)
- Research and planning (started after the season ends, but can also be done throughout the season)

## Ambient Observables
- Sunlight/artificial light
	- Lux (lumens per unit area)
	- Time
	- Angles (azimuthal and polar)
- Wind
	- Intensity 
	- Direction
- Rainfall
- Soil texture (ratio of silt : sand : loam)
- Soil Ph (acid, neutral, or basic)
- Hygroscopicity (how well soil holds water)
- Water content (wetness)
- Mineral content (amount of compounds with nitrogen, phosphorous, calcium, potassium, copper, iron, zinc, molybdenum, sulfur, and other ionic species that exist in the soil)
- Humidity
- Pressure
- Temperature
- Space (distance between plantings; size of beds; dimensions of paths, compost, and other things)

#### Soil amending
This activity requires digging up the soil and adding solid fertilizers, compost, or biochar. As such, it uses similar tools. Essentially, this step is like fertilizing but by putting it under the topsoil.

#### Fertilizing
This is a fairly straightforward process whose tools depend on the fertilizer being spread. Sometimes, you can just use your hand. Other times, you need to dilute a liquid fertilizer to be delivered during watering. 
- Square shovel (can also just use a spade)
- Gloves (to prevent chemical irritation with some commercial fertilizers)

#### Research and planning
if you are using physical media to plan out your garden space a drafting table helps

If you are doing it online, then there are tools which can help you plan out your garden beds, look how to intercrop/companion plant effectively, and easily share these results with friends and comrades
- https://planter.garden/ -> My mom uses this to good success. It's a simple tool that can help set a baseline for how you are going to approach your garden. Since the app seems to be pretty comprehensive in terms of how much labor it takes out of the planning process, I'd recommend using a second source for confirming companion plantings and being extra careful not to plant too densely. 

#### Building
There are a million tools for building things. I wouldn't get bogged down in trying to get a lot, and it depends on a case-by-case basis what tools you might need. However, if you are going to be building some garden beds, a greenhouse, compost bins, [[01Action plans#Action Item 3 Biochar and Vermicomposting|Worm bins]], or other basic structures, having the following will be handy:
- Electric drill (non-impact)
- Impact drill
- Drill bit 
- Circular saw
- Jigsaw
- Screws and fasteners (different lengths; rated for outdoor use)
- Beater screwdriver (beat this with a hammer for use like a chisel)
- Measuring tape
- Hammer
- Level
- Marker
- Vices
- Workbench (can be a slab of wood and two saw horses)
- Pliers
- Sawsall (with metal and wood blades)

These tools will get you pretty far in terms of being able to build most structures that don't use fancy bonding techniques or niche materials other than wood, dirt, thread, metal wire, PVC, and some metals. If the list seems a bit too extensive and overwhelming, consider funding a tool library with your friends.
