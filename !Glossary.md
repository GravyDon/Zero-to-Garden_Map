###### Allelopathic
The property of producing biochemicals present within the soil matrix which, in high enough concentrations, can inhibit the biological functions of other plants including the plant producing the allelopathic chemicals. Allelopathic plants should be planted with caution and only in places where the produced chemicals will not seriously effect the ecology. However, there do exist certain allelopathic plants whose biochemicals can be broken down via the action of microorganisms or other plants. Examples of allelopathic plants are: black walnut, tree of heaven, black crowberry, spotted knapweed, garlic mustard, and nut grass. 
Not to be confused with allopathic.
###### *Rhizosphere* 
**The zone around a plant that consists of roots, symbiotic relationships with soil fauna, and the soil that is subject to drain water through the root volume.** The rhizosphere is relevant in the consideration of what region of the soil a plant's below-ground anatomy is subject to absorbing the surrounding, soluble compounds in the soil. Extension past the root zone in what is considered the rhizosphere is possible when interactions between fungi, bacteria, and macro-fauna are involved which deliver nutrients to the root zone. 

###### Plant Hardiness Zone:
The zones that are demarcated based on latitude and geographical effects on annual extreme temperatures. Zones are classified by number and letter, with the number indicating the major temperature categories and letters indicating minor, intranumeral differences in temperature not worthy of numeric reclassification. Usage of zones and zone maps are for feasibility for planting perennials, biennials, and otherwise persistent plants that will have to endure winter extremes. Plants are described to be "hardy up to zone \_\_"; the higher the number, the warmer the winter temperatures. The higher the number, the closer to the equator the region is. Zones with high numbers typically support tropical plants and any plants which can stand to compete with native tropical varieties. Zones with lower numbers are restricted to plants which are adapted to the extreme cold or will have to sheltered using a greenhouse and heating technology.

###### Hardy
Also see: hardening off (verb), hardiness (adj)
The qualitative measurement of how tolerant a particular plant or plant grouping is to cold temperatures. Hardiness comes in different classifications. for example PFAF (plants for a future) uses hardiness levels 1-4: 1 being not hardy at all to 4 being hardy even in the coldest temperatures. Zone maps use hardiness zones which classify which plants are hardy in which zones. Hardiness essentially gives a range of extreme, low temperatures that any given plant will survive.

###### Soil colloids/Soil colloidal solution
Soil colloids are the main constituents that are absorbed by plants from the aqueoous phase; this colloidal phase is the interplay between the minerological, organic, water, and gaseous phases. **The value in understanding the thermodynamics and chemical potentials possible depending on the surrounding solid phase is the core of what makes a good soil.**
